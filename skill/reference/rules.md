## LLM cliches — `llm-cliches`

Stock phrasings and rhythms that language models overproduce. Collected by Simon Willison for the LLM cliche highlighter.

| rule | flags | fix |
|---|---|---|
| `no-chain` | Two or more “no …” items in a row, e.g. “No fluff, no filler, no jargon.” The badge counts the “no” items. | Say what it does have. A denial chain lists absences instead of substance. |
| `whole` | “That / this is the whole point, game, thing …” | Name the thing directly instead of gesturing at its totality. |
| `did-not-chain` | Two or more “did not …” or “didn’t …” items in a row. The badge counts the items. | Collapse the negations into one concrete statement of what happened. |
| `dont-verb-it` | “Don’t call it X. Call it Y.” — a negated verb + “it”, then the same verb + “it” again. | Drop the setup and keep the reframing, or cut both. |
| `sit-with` | The reflective “sit with that / this / it (for a moment)”, plus “sit with the discomfort” and friends. | Say what the reader should conclude, not that they should dwell. |
| `already-know` | “You already know” — the answer, what to do, or standing alone before a full stop. | Either state the answer or ask a real question. Do not flatter. |
| `is-the-entire` | “X is the entire point / game / business model.” | Replace the totalising claim with the specific mechanism. |
| `the-entire-is` | “The entire point / game / business model is …” — the flipped twin of “is the entire”. | Replace the totalising claim with the specific mechanism. |
| `is-real` | “The X is real, and / not …”, including “is the real … and it”. Skips “real estate”, “real time”, and similar. | Cut "is real". Give the evidence that makes it real. |
| `punchline` | “The punchline is …”, “the punchline:”, or “the punchline?”. | Deliver the point without announcing that a point is coming. |
| `worth-naming` | The therapist-voiced “that loss is real and it’s worth naming”, “it’s worth naming that …”, or a “Worth naming:” opener. Skips “naming names”. | Name it, or cut it. Announcing that something deserves naming is not naming it. |
| `not-nothing` | “That is not nothing” / “that’s not nothing”, plus the “this / it / which is not nothing” variants. | Quantify it. "Not nothing" concedes and asserts at once, saying neither. |
| `is-the-whole` | Any subject + “is the whole point / trick / pitch / idea”, plus the “here is the whole …” opener. The twin of “is the entire …”, and a generalisation  | Name the thing directly instead of gesturing at its totality. |
| `echo-triad` | Consecutive sentences built on the same repeated skeleton — “A shopping cart is an object in the system. A chat room is an object in the system.” The  | Merge the parallel sentences into one, or vary the structure. |
| `performative-honesty` | Sincerity announced rather than demonstrated: “I won’t pretend”, “I’ll be honest”, “let’s be honest”, “to be clear”, and sentence-initial “Honestly,”  | Delete the preamble. Honest writing does not announce itself. |
| `thats-the-part` | Gesturing at a favoured detail instead of stating it: “that is the part a counter can’t reach”, “the part that makes me trust the rest”, “my favourite | State the detail instead of pointing at it. |
| `the-only-i-trust` | The narrowing superlative reveal: “the only marketing I trust”, “the only thing it needs”, “the only X that matters”. | Drop the superlative framing and give the actual reason. |
| `take-my-word` | The stock invitation to verify: “you don’t have to take my word for it”, “don’t take my word for any of this”. | Just link the evidence. |
| `turns-out` | The casual-revelation opener, almost always bolted to a tidy conclusion: “Turns out X”, “it turns out that X”. | State the finding without the casual-reveal framing. |
| `fits-in-your-head` | Dev-blog boilerplate for simplicity: “small enough to hold in your head”, “batteries included”, “it just works”, “zero config”, “sane defaults”. | Give the concrete measure - lines of code, number of concepts, setup steps. |
| `stacked-questions` | Two or more questions fired in a row, usually fragments after the first: “Do I know how it works? Where it breaks? Which corners it cut?” The badge co | Keep one question, or convert them to statements. |
| `sentence-anaphora` | Three or more consecutive sentences starting on the same word — “Maybe nobody needed it. Maybe it introduced … Maybe a small convenience …” Pronouns a | Vary the sentence openings or combine into one sentence. |
| `colon-triple` | A colon opening onto three or more comma-separated prose items: “separate ports, processes, and local state”. The most common shape LLM prose uses to  | Fine in a real list. Suspect when it is prose padding out to three items. |
| `heres-the-twist` | The stage-managed reveal: “here’s the twist”, “here’s the thing”, “here’s the catch / kicker / rub”, “here’s the first example:”. | Cut the stage direction and give the content. |
| `x-is-dead` | The obituary headline and its sequel: “peer code review is dead”, “botd is dead; long live botd”. | Say what changed and for whom, instead of the obituary headline. |
| `thats-why-mattered` | Retroactively assigning significance: “that’s why being able to open the environment mattered”, “this is why preserving every conversation mattered”. | State the consequence directly rather than asserting significance. |
| `stranded-auxiliary` | A clause that lands on a bare auxiliary for the reversal: “The tool died; the data didn’t.”, “Reading mostly passed … Writing didn’t”, “Maybe it would | Complete the clause. The bare auxiliary is a rhythm trick, not information. |

## Signs of AI writing (Wikipedia) — `wikipedia-ai`

Tells catalogued by Wikipedia editors reviewing AI-generated article text.

| rule | flags | fix |
|---|---|---|
| `ai-vocab` | Words LLMs lean on far more than people do: “delve”, “tapestry”, “meticulous”, “pivotal”, “intricate”, “interplay”, “underscore”, “garner”, “bolster”, | Swap for a plainer word. One hit is coincidence; several is a tell. |
| `not-just` | Negative parallelisms: “not just X, but (also) Y”, “not only … but …”, and the “it’s not X — it’s Y” contrast. | Pick the accurate half. Negative parallelism inflates without adding. |
| `note-that` | Didactic hedging: “it is important to note that”, “it’s worth noting”, “it should be noted”, plus the “worth pausing / considering / asking” family. | Delete the hedge and state the fact. |
| `testament` | “Stands / serves as a testament (or reminder)”, “is a testament to” — inflating significance instead of saying what happened. | Say what happened instead of what it supposedly proves. |
| `crucial-role` | “Plays a crucial / pivotal / vital / key / significant role in …”. | Say what it actually does. |
| `landscape` | Scene-setting boilerplate: “the ever-evolving / changing / shifting landscape”, “in today’s fast-paced world”. | Cut the scene-setting and start with the subject. |
| `vague-experts` | Vague attribution to unnamed authorities: “experts argue”, “some critics have noted”, “observers suggest”, “industry reports indicate”. | Name the source, or drop the claim. |
| `despite-challenges` | The boilerplate challenges-and-outlook formula: “despite these challenges”, “faces several challenges”, “challenges remain”, “remains to be seen”, “ti | Replace the formula with the specific challenge and outcome. |
| `participle-tail` | Superficial analysis bolted onto a sentence end: “…, highlighting / underscoring / showcasing / reflecting the …”. | End the sentence. The participle tail adds commentary, not information. |
| `promo` | Travel-brochure tone: “nestled in”, “in the heart of”, “rich tapestry / heritage”, “hidden gem”, “boasts a”, “breathtaking”, “stunning views”. | Drop the brochure register and describe plainly. |
| `ai-leftovers` | Artifacts pasted straight from a chatbot: “as an AI language model”, “as of my last update”, “knowledge cutoff”, plus markup debris like “oaicite”, “c | Remove the artifact. This is chatbot output pasted verbatim. |
