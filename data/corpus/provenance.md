# Where the human half came from

Every human text here was published before generative writing tools existed, and the
date was taken from the publisher's own machinery rather than from a page's visible
text wherever one was available: a JATS `<pub-date>` element, a content API, a git
commit, a PDF imprint, an arXiv Atom record. What follows is the collection note for
each register, kept as written at collection time.

Every date is between 2017 and 2018, five to nine years before the collection date and well before 2022. Cleaning: HTML was unescaped, <p> tags converted to paragraph breaks, all other tags removed, and internal whitespace normalised. For forum-3 the comment's opening paragraph was wrapped in <i> tags in the source HTML, which on Hacker News marks quoted material from the linked article; that paragraph was deleted so the file contains only the commenter's own prose. No bullet lists, numbered lists, code blocks, block quotes or URLs survive; the parenthetical (1)-(4) sequence in forum-1 is inline within a single sentence rather than a set-out list.

*Confidence.* High for all three. The strongest evidence is structural rather than stylistic: all three were published years before any generally available language model could have produced text of this length and coherence, and both the Algolia index and the independent Firebase API agree on the timestamps, so the dates are not self-reported by the pages. Internally the texts also read as human. forum-1 is a specific personal anecdote with idiosyncratic detail (the pizza restaurants, the rumoured Mustang), a couple of genuine grammatical slips ('no where near work', 'The cops found no drugs and any of his lots'), and an aside marked LOL. forum-2 argues by loose analogy and self-interrupting asides, includes an invented investor dialogue, and uses a placeholder like $XXX million. forum-3 is the most formal of the three and reads as careful professional argument, but it is written in the first person from a specific career history and carries a distinctive, slightly repetitive cadence; it comes from a Hacker News account with a long public posting history in that voice. The only caveat worth recording is authenticity of authorship rather than of date: forum-1 and forum-3 are personal-experience claims that cannot be externally verified, and any HN comment could in principle be text a user copied from elsewhere - but in either case it remains pre-2022 human prose, which is what the measurement requires.
